# Research: What Top Small / Boutique Law Firm Websites Do Well

**Firm context:** Crawford Law PLLC (Texas SOS File No. 806712661; effective 07/24/2026). Principal: Andrew Michael Crawford, J.D., SPHR (IL & TX), Leander, TX 78641. Specialty focus: employer-side employment law (workplace investigations, FMLA/ADA accommodations & leave, training, labor relations). Secondary: general counsel-style work (contracts, formations, family law, estate planning). Existing bio/services donor: [crawfordlawfinance.com](https://crawfordlawfinance.com/).

**Research date:** July 27, 2026

---

## Executive summary

High-performing small-firm sites are not digital brochures. They behave like **intake engines**: clear who-you-serve positioning in seconds, dedicated practice-area pages for high-intent search, trust signals that match buyer anxiety, and a low-friction path from first click → consultation → file open.

For Crawford Law, the closest “best in class” pattern is **employer-side boutique employment** (Perez Law, Treaty Oak Employers’ Legal Group, Muskat Devine), not generic solo general-practice templates. General-practice pages still matter for local community demand, but they should sit in a **secondary track** so the brand does not read as “we do everything” — which weakens both SEO and credibility with HR/employer buyers.

## Benchmark firms & patterns

### 1. Perez Law LLC — closest specialty analog
**URL:** [perezlawllc.com](https://perezlawllc.com/)

**What works**
- Instant clarity: “workplace investigations, workforce training and employment law.”
- Practice framed as three products employers understand: **Retainer / Training / Investigations**.
- Proof stack: Chambers, Best Lawyers, testimonials from HR leaders (not generic 5-star blurbs).
- Positioning as extension of the HR team / predictable spend — language employers buy.

**Takeaway for Crawford:** Lead with productized employment offerings (Investigations, Leave & Accommodations, Training, Ongoing Employer Counsel), not a flat list of statutes.

### 2. Treaty Oak Employers’ Legal Group (Austin area)
**URL:** [treatyoaklegal.com](https://treatyoaklegal.com/)

**What works**
- Hero is problem-first: “My employee…” scenarios → “Now what?”
- Service buckets map to urgency: Workplace Investigations / Employment Emergencies / Prevention & Compliance.
- Local authority claim for investigations in Central Texas.
- Strong CTA psychology for employers mid-crisis (the primary high-intent buyer).

**Takeaway for Crawford:** Homepage and investigation pages should speak to **the moment of need**, then route calm/planning buyers to training and retainers.

### 3. Muskat Devine (Houston L&E boutique)
**URL:** [muskatdevine.com](https://muskatdevine.com/)

**What works**
- Ruthless focus: “Labor and employment law is all we do.”
- Brand voice: business-minded, responsive, practical — attributes employers reward.

**Caveat:** Pure specialty focus is ideal for SEO, but Crawford intentionally offers broader services. Do **not** copy “we only do employment” literally; instead use a **primary specialty + secondary track** IA (see architecture plan).

| Pattern | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| One page per practice area (not one dump page) | Matches search intent; converts better |
| Case-type / subtopic pages under hubs | Captures long-tail + AI question queries |
| Practice pages as “solution hubs” | Pain → process → proof → CTA |

## What “professional + attention-getting” actually means for employers

Employer-side visitors (HR, owners, GCs) evaluate differently from consumer plaintiffs:

1. **Competence & discretion** over drama or “fight for you” language.
2. **Process clarity** (investigation steps, leave interactive process, training formats).
3. **Speed & availability** (“can you help this week?”).
4. **Credentials & Texas jurisdiction** (State Bar, PLLC, Leander / Williamson County / Austin metro).
5. **Predictable engagement model** (consult → scope → engagement letter → portal).

Consumer/general-practice visitors (family, estate, small business formation) need:
- Plain-language reassurance
- Local trust (“Leander / Cedar Park attorney”)
- Easy scheduling
- Clear fee/consultation expectations

**Design implication:** One brand, two visitor modes — with shared professionalism, different copy and CTAs.

---

## Anti-patterns to avoid

- Homepage that lists 8–12 equal practice areas (reads as generalist mill).
- Thin city pages (“We proudly serve Leander…”) with no local substance.
- Single contact form for every matter type with 20+ fields.
- Stock courthouse imagery + Inter/Roboto purple-gradient legal template look.
- Hiding the attorney behind firm abstraction — solos convert on **named trust**.
- Separate FAQ-only hub that cannibalizes practice-area pages for AI citation.

---

## Design / UX principles for Crawford’s landing page

Aligned with high-end boutique + conversion research:

1. **One composition in the first viewport:** brand (Crawford Law PLLC), one sharp headline, one supporting line, one CTA group, one dominant visual (attorney / office / real workplace context — not collage cards).
2. **Brand-first:** Firm name must survive the “remove the nav” test.
3. **Immediate dual path after hero scroll or secondary CTA:** Employers vs Business & Personal matters.
4. **Trust strip below fold:** Texas PLLC, Leander, practice focus, State Bar link — not floating badges over the hero.
5. **Motion with purpose:** subtle entrance of headline/CTA; sticky mobile “Schedule consult” — not decorative noise.
